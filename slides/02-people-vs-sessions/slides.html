Lesson 2 · People vs sessions

How many people
used your app?

The easiest question in analytics, and almost nobody answers it right.

A simple question

Stride, a fitness app, had a good month.

1,240

visits in its database. How many people is that?

Write your guess down before the next slide.

Three things get called "users"

Each counts something different

A visit

One time the app was opened. Also called a session. The same person makes many.

A browser

One device and one browser, remembered by a stored id. Clear it, or open a private window, and it's "new".

An account

Someone who signed in. The closest thing to a person, but only for people who sign in.

A browser is not a person

It breaks in both directions

One person, two devices

Maya checks Stride on her phone in the morning and on her laptop at work. Two browsers. One Maya. You count too many.

Two people, one device

A couple share the tablet on the kitchen counter. One browser. Two people. You count too few.

Signing in is what lets you tell them apart: the same account on two browsers, or two accounts on one.

Some visits aren't visits

Before you count, decide what's in

  • Your own test runs. You opened the app forty times while building it.
  • Another app's rows. Two studies, one table.
  • Visits with no clicks. Opened the link, left. A visit, but did they use the app?

None of these is "wrong data". Each is a decision, and the decision belongs next to the number.

None is a lie. They answer different questions. The lie is printing one under the wrong label.

Principle · Of whom?

"40% came back" means nothing until you can say:
40% of what?

Maya on her phone, then her laptop, looks like two new visitors who never returned. Count by browser and she lowers your return rate. Count by account and she raises it.

Define before you count

Every number gets one sentence

We count people, from visits to Stride in May, excluding our test runs, because they aren't users.

If you can't fill the blanks, you don't know what your number means yet. Neither does your AI assistant.

Principle · Follow one person

Before you trust a total, trace one row

  • Pick one browser. List its visits.
  • Did it sign in? As which account? On which other browsers does that account appear?
  • Count that one person by hand, then check your dashboard counts them the same way.

If one person comes out right, the total might be right. If one comes out wrong, the total is wrong.

Your guided walkthrough

Build "Who used the app?"

  • Load the lesson's practice data into your database.
  • On your dashboard, show visits, browsers, people and how many came back.
  • Under every number: its one-sentence definition.
  • Answer the questions. Then write the memo.

The practice data has traps in it. The questions know where. They won't tell you.

Lesson 2 · Summary

What you learnt

  • Visits, browsers, accounts and people are four different counts. "Users" is a choice.
  • One person on two devices inflates the count; two people on one device deflate it. Sign-ins tell them apart.
  • Test runs, other apps' rows and visits with no taps are decisions to state, not dirt to hide.
  • Every number carries its sentence: we count …, from …, excluding …, because …
  • Before trusting a total, trace one person from the raw rows to the dashboard.
