Lesson 1 · The data you start with

What did the app
actually write down?

Every number in this course is made of raw rows. Know them before you count them.

A simple question

Noa spends three minutes in Lumo, a reading app.

She scrolls the home page, opens two articles, reads one to the end, saves it, and leaves.

What does Lumo's database know about those three minutes?

Write your answer down before the next slide.

Recorded vs not

If it isn't a row, it didn't happen

Recorded

The visit itself: when, which device and browser, which link she came from.
Every tap: which screen, where on it, what it landed on.
Every choice the app reports: what, and after how long.
A sign-in, as a scrambled account id.

Not recorded

What she looked at. How far she scrolled. That she read the article to the end. What she thought of it. Anything between two taps.

"She read it" is not in the data. "She opened it, and 4 minutes passed before her next tap" is.

What a database is

Six words you need

session_idplatformstarted_at
a41f…mobile10:02:01
e19b…null10:11:20
clicks: session_idstep
a41f…home
a41f…article

Table: one kind of thing (visits). Row: one of them. Column: one fact about each.

Key: the column that names a row, session_id. No two rows share it.

Type: what a column holds: textnumbertimetrue/false. null means "not known", never zero or "no".

Join: a tap carries its visit's key, so one visit links to many taps. Following that link brings them together.

Four tables

One visit, and everything that happened in it

sessions
a visit

device, browser, source link, start time, and two flags: test run, practice data

clicks
a tap

the screen, x and y in the app's frame, what it hit (none = nothing)

selections
a choice

the screen, what was chosen, how long the screen was open before it

identities
a sign-in

a scrambled id: same account, same value, never the email

Every row of the last three carries the visit's session_id. That is how you put a visit back together.

Four misreadings

The columns that look like something else

user_id

Looks like a person. It is a browser. Noa on her laptop tomorrow is a new one.

a sessions row

Looks like someone using the app. It is a visit, and some visits have no taps at all.

is_test · is_synthetic

Look like the same flag. Test = your own runs. Synthetic = the course's practice data. Real visits: both false.

app

Looks like it's always yours. It is whichever app wrote the row. Two studies can share a table.

Principle · Capture

The page loaded. No error appeared.
Zero rows were saved.

Lumo changed one database rule on a Friday. For two weeks every tap was silently refused, and the app looked perfect. The only proof capture works: write a row, then find it.

Who may do what

Nobody can damage collected data, including you

The app

may add rows. Never read, change or delete them.

You, signed in

may read rows. Never change or delete them.

Your analysis

is built on top, as views in its own space. The raw rows stay as they were recorded.

The same goes for your AI assistant. It cannot "clean up" a row it didn't like.

Two spaces

The raw tables, and your views on top

Raw tables

The four tables the app writes into. Exactly what was recorded, never changed. The same for every team.

Your views, in analysis

A view is a saved question: "the photo visits, without test runs". It holds no data of its own. Ask it, and it reads the raw tables at that moment, so it is never out of date.

Everything you build in this course is a view. The raw tables are the evidence; the views are your reading of it.

Principle · One definition, one place

Lumo's dashboard had three numbers called "readers"

812

built in week 2, from visits

655

built in week 5, excluding test runs

590

built in week 7, from accounts

None was wrong. Nobody knew which one the board report used. Rule: every number is a view, carrying its one-sentence definition. A second definition of "readers" is a second view, with a different name.

Lesson 1 · Summary

What you learnt

  • The app records four things: visits, taps, choices and sign-ins. Anything else, it doesn't know.
  • A visit is put back together through its session_id, in order of time.
  • user_id is a browser, not a person. is_test and is_synthetic mark different rows. app says whose row it is.
  • Capture is proven by writing a row and finding it, never by the absence of an error.
  • Every number is a view with its definition, so the database does the counting.
