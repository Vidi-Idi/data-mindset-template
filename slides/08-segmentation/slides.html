Lesson 8 · Segmentation

Who does better —
and is it real?

Splitting your users into groups is how you find out what's going on. It's also the fastest way to fool yourself.

One number hides many groups

A segment is a rule that splits people

By device

Mobile or desktop.

By source

Where they came from: the link they clicked, the ad, the email.

By history

First visit or returning. New account or old.

Good segments are chosen before you look at the results. Segments chosen after are usually the ones that told the story you wanted.

Within every source, mobile does better. Overall, mobile does worse. Both are true.

How can both be true?

The groups aren't mixed the same way

70%
of mobile visits

come from the TikTok ad, the weakest source.

19%
of desktop visits

come from the TikTok ad. Most come from Google, the strongest.

This is called Simpson's paradox. The overall gap isn't about devices at all. It's about where each device's traffic comes from.

Small groups lie

The best segment is often the tiniest

Forko's newsletter readers on mobile: 9 visits, 8 checkouts. 89%.

One more visit that doesn't check out, and it's 80%. Two, and it's 73%.

Decide a minimum size before you look, and show the size next to every rate. A rate without its count is half a number.

Principle · Same name, same thing?

Check your labels before you split on them

One thing, two names

Half the Instagram links say instagram, half say ig. Split by source and Instagram becomes two smaller, weaker-looking segments.

No name at all

Some visits have no device recorded. They aren't mobile or desktop. Drop them silently and every platform number shifts.

A segment is a promise

Apply it everywhere, or say where you didn't

You pick "mobile only". The headline changes. The chart below it doesn't. The table at the bottom half does.

Nothing on the screen is wrong on its own. Together they describe three different groups of people, and the reader can't tell.

Every number on the page should say which segment it shows.

Lesson 8 · Summary

What you learnt

  • A segment is a rule, chosen before you look at the results.
  • A gap overall can reverse inside every segment (Simpson's paradox). Look at how each group is made up.
  • A rate without its count is half a number. Set a minimum size in advance.
  • Check a label's real values first: one source under two names, values that are missing.
  • A segment applies to every number on the page, or the page says where it doesn't.
